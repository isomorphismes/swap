# David Madore: transitive permutation groups on six objects

## Reference and credit

- **David Madore**, [*Transitive permutation groups on six objects*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9KRVK1_2QXs) (video and animation). Thank you, David Madore, for the clear visual demonstration.
- Madore's description: up to conjugacy, **16 transitive permutation subgroups of** (S_6). He illustrates each by selecting elements of the chosen group and moving **six colored disks**. The examples range from (S_6), which can realize every permutation, to the cyclic group (C_6), generated by a six-cycle.
- Related but distinct, later reference: [Travis's interactive visual explanation of the exceptional outer automorphism of (S_6)](https://travisdoesmath.github.io/s6/), *The Weird Symmetry of Shuffling Six Items*. Thank you to the author for the interactive construction. Its six-object action is an **additional** visualization idea, not the subject of Madore's video.

This is a **design/reference note**, not evidence that Swap implements a six-object scene, all 16 groups, or an outer automorphism.

## Relation to Swap

Swap already begins with **three fixed-colored braid identities**, their visible signed crossings, and a rigid equilateral triangle carrying corresponding identity colors. Keep that scene. Madore supplies a complementary **finite permutation-group action** scene.

The mathematical relationship should be made explicit, not erased:

[
B_n \twoheadrightarrow S_n, \qquad
\sigma_i\mapsto (i\ i+1), \qquad
\sigma_i^{-1}\mapsto (i\ i+1).
]

A permutation records where identities end up; the braid records **how they got there**, including the sign and order of crossings. For example, (sigma_i^2) has the identity endpoint permutation but is not the identity braid. Consequently, a six-disk permutation animation can be synchronized to a braid lift, but the two data types must remain separate. A group element in (S_6) has many braid lifts; an arbitrary choice of lift must not be mistaken for the group's intrinsic motion.

## Possible six-disk view

1. Display **six permanent slots** (perhaps equally spaced on a circle) and **six permanently colored disk identities**. Render an identity's motion between slots, not a spontaneous exchange of its color. The slots and identities are different kinds of objects.
2. Select a specified subgroup (G\le S_6) with verified generators or elements. An element (g\in G) acts on the current ordering, and six paths animate the corresponding movement. In parallel display cycle notation, current slot/identity mapping, and optionally the selected generator word.
3. Provide **step, replay, pause**, and a choice between showing an arbitrary element of (G) and a particular generator. Mark whether the sequence is a uniformly sampled group element or a random walk on generators: the latter does not automatically sample uniformly.
4. As an optional linked pane, decompose the permutation into adjacent transpositions and animate **one chosen braid lift**, with explicit over/under signs. Never infer the crossing sign from the final permutation.
5. An optional orbit highlight can make *transitivity* concrete: for each disk identity, there exists some element of (G) that sends it to any of the six slots. Transitivity does **not** mean a single element moves all six disks or that every ordering is reachable under (G).

Suggested explorations start with familiar groups (C_6), a dihedral hexagon action, and (S_6); the complete sixteen conjugacy classes could become a gallery after the underlying group actions and six-slot animation work. The original three-object (S_3) action is the natural bridge from Swap's existing triangle.

## Correctness boundaries

- Distinguish **abstract group**, its **specific degree-six permutation representation**, a selected **element**, the resulting **permutation**, and an optional **braid lift**. Two isomorphic groups need not have equivalent six-point actions.
- Composition order must be consistent between the displayed word, the computed permutation, and the animation (especially when actions are replayed).
- A generated permutation must preserve all six identities exactly once. Verify the generator set defines the intended subgroup; do not generate six-disk shuffles from the whole (S_6) while claiming a smaller group.
- If showing a motion as six arbitrary smooth paths, call it an **animation of the permutation**, not a mathematically canonical braid. If crossings are meaningful, record them explicitly.
- Keep the tactile three-strand/triangle experience available; the six-disk gallery extends rather than replaces it.

## Further mathematical possibilities

The exceptional outer automorphism of (S_6) suggests a much later, unusually visual topic: two inequivalent six-object permutation actions can be related through the outer automorphism. It is **not needed** to imitate Madore's demonstration and should not inflate the initial six-disk scene.
