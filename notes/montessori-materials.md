# Montessori and adjacent mathematical toys: photographic references for Swap

Research notes, 2026-10-09. These are **identified candidates**, not a claim that
we know which particular toy the user saw. This is a reference collection, not
a requirement to reproduce Montessori lessons or to adopt their exact colors.

## Recalled physical and mathematical observations

- A kindergarten activity with many removable pegs/cylinders that children lift,
  move, sort, and replace. The interesting features were size, length,
  continuous quantity, and possibly area.
- An ordered family of approximately twelve bars in the user's recollection,
  demonstrating relations such as `1 + 2 = 3` through **relative lengths**,
  independently of the physical choice of a unit.
- Pieces move through space instead of simply changing labels. Their paths can
  become colored braid strands, initially three. The three-strand triangle
  provides a second, linked geometric view.
- Related explorations: Pascal's triangle, branching left/right routes,
  probability, and later higher-dimensional arrangements. Those are ideas for
  Swap, not claims about historical Montessori equipment.

## Closest matches: original Montessori sensorial and mathematical materials

### 1. Knobbed cylinder blocks — very plausible for “pull out and move pegs”

![A Montessori knobbed cylinder block and its removable cylinders](https://somontessori.com/2011-thickbox_default/bloc-de-cylindres-n1.jpg)

Ten wooden cylinders are lifted by small knobs and returned to correspondingly
sized holes. Different blocks vary height, diameter, or both. A mismatched
placement leaves a visible/physical error. The essential interaction is **remove,
compare, reinsert**. Some commercial Montessori-style pegboards look different:
a square grid of exposed brightly colored pegs is not automatically one of
these traditional cylinder blocks.

Sources:
- [American Montessori Society: the knobbed cylinders](https://amshq.org/blog/uncategorized/2023-01-20-montessori-knobbed-cylinders-introduction/)
- [Montessori Album: knobbed cylinders and four blocks](https://www.montessorialbum.com/montessori/index.php/Knobbed_Cylinders)
- [So Montessori: photograph of cylinder block](https://somontessori.com/sensoriel/bloc-de-cylindres-n1)

### 2. Knobless cylinders — plausible for loose, movable pegs

![Four boxes of Montessori knobless cylinders, red, green, yellow and blue](https://absorbentminds.co.uk/cdn/shop/products/s1115j_1_18851439-b93d-405a-9e34-32813cdcaff7.jpg?v=1668167777&width=1445)

Four sets of ten colored cylinders, graduated by height and diameter. Unlike
socketed cylinders, these are freely ordered, stacked, and compared. They
represent a **different sort of freedom**: the same dimension discrimination
without enforcing a unique destination hole.

Sources:
- [American Montessori Society: section on knobless cylinders](https://amshq.org/blog/uncategorized/2023-01-20-montessori-knobbed-cylinders-introduction/)
- [Absorbent Minds: photograph](https://absorbentminds.co.uk/products/knobless-cylinders-1)

### 3. Red Rods — strongest candidate for pre-numerical relative length

![Ten Montessori red rods graduated by length](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/61962fa007d00204fab6f9fe/1668783460618-B1BTHZC8IV7CRYX8J46K/RR.jpeg)

Ten red rods, lengths 10, 20, ..., 100 cm, with common cross-section.
Their purpose is discriminating **length**, without marking discrete count
units. One can align rods or compare endpoints without introducing numerals.

Sources:
- [Nienhuis Montessori: Red Rods, AMI-approved](https://www.nienhuis.com/us/product/the-red-rods/500_002600/)
- [Montessori Together: photograph](https://montessoritogether.com/montessori-1-2/blog-post-title-four-gmbnw-dzpy5-5y4sk-c67jj-45pnw)

### 4. Number Rods — closest traditional Montessori candidate for visible arithmetic

![Montessori number rods with red and blue unit segments](https://www.pangealekoglaer.no/cdn/shop/files/FullSizeRender_411411e4-d3cd-4c01-a1f6-2ca28a2215fe.jpg?v=1727191317&width=1920)

Also ten rods of length 10–100 cm, but red/blue alternating segments
make the integer units countable. A one-rod and a two-rod aligned end to end
have the length of a three-rod. Both an overall continuous length and a
segmentation into discrete units remain visible.

Sources:
- [Nienhuis Montessori: Number Rods, AMI-approved](https://www.nienhuis.com/us/product/number-rods/500_002700/)
- [Montessori Album: photograph of 1 + 2 = 3](https://www.montessorialbum.com/montessori/index.php?title=File%3AAddition_with_Number_Rods_6.JPG)
- [Pangea: photograph](https://www.pangealekoglaer.no/en/products/tallstaver)

### 5. Colored bead stair — discrete numbers become lengths

The traditional short bead stair arranges quantities 1–10 as differently
colored bead bars. The sequence forms a triangular/staircase silhouette.
This is another distinct possibility if the remembered “bars” were strings
of beads rather than solid wooden rods.

Sources:
- [Trillium Montessori: Short Bead Stair](https://www.trilliummontessori.org/montessori-math/)
- [Global Montessori Network: colored bead bars](https://theglobalmontessorinetwork.org/resource/elementary/small-bead-chain-color/)

### 6. Constructive Triangles — relevant to Swap's triangle, not yet an identification

![Montessori constructive triangles in wooden cases](https://ifitmontessori.ca/cdn/shop/files/SEN-038.jpg?v=1728078766)

Independent colored triangle pieces are turned, flipped, aligned, and joined to
create composite geometric figures. The important visual device is that
**orientation and boundary identification matter**: a triangular piece is a
thing one moves, not a shape whose colors are simply reassigned.

Sources:
- [IFIT Montessori: image and five-box set](https://ifitmontessori.ca/products/constructive-triangles-5-boxes-sen-038)
- [Montessori Materials: constructive triangle operations](https://www.montessorimaterials.net/sensorial/constructive-triangles)

### 7. Multiplication board — candidate if the remembered pegs were numerous tiny counters

![A Montessori multiplication board with red beads in a grid](https://absorbentminds.co.uk/cdn/shop/products/m1413jm_a7d00eae-62ab-4730-93f7-4c84d335d0a7.jpg?v=1667753511&width=1445)

A 10-by-10 grid of holes accepts small red beads/counters. A child builds
rectangular arrays by putting counters into holes. This may resemble a
“many pegs that children move” activity, but it is **not** the same exercise
as graded cylinders or lengths.

Source: [Absorbent Minds Montessori: multiplication board and image](https://absorbentminds.co.uk/products/multiplication-board)

## Related material, explicitly NOT original Montessori

### 8. Cuisenaire rods — especially good match for equalities between lengths

![Colored Cuisenaire rods arranged to show elementary sums](https://http2.mlstatic.com/D_NQ_NP_681362-MEC88488988064_072025-O.webp)

Georges Cuisenaire's separate system uses lengths 1–10 in unit increments,
with a distinct color for each length (e.g., white=1, red=2, light green=3).
Arrange 1 and 2 end-to-end next to 3 to show the equality of total lengths.
This is **not** an original Montessori apparatus, though it is often grouped
with hands-on Montessori-style manipulatives.

Sources:
- [Miquon Math: origin and mechanics of Cuisenaire rods](https://miquonmath.com/about/cuisenaire-rods/)
- [Example photograph of Cuisenaire arithmetic](https://www.mercadolibre.com.ec/regletas-cuisenaire-de-100-piezas/up/MECU3324774185)

### 9. Galton board / bean machine — link to Pascal rivers, not Montessori

![Galton board with triangular peg array and falling red beads](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/57de6ebc414fb546195be85a/1541775553499-MPBL6NP2O2QQFQWM8PJM/Galton%2BBoard%2B%28Binostat/Bean%2BBoard%29%2B-%2BSTEMpunkED)

Balls fall through triangular arrays of pins, each deflection taking one of
two directions. The count of possible routes is Pascal's triangle and binomial
coefficients; frequencies approach a normal shape after proper scaling.
This is relevant to the previously discussed branching red/blue rivers, not
evidence that the Montessori toy was a Galton board.

Sources:
- [STEMpunkED: photograph of physical board](https://www.stempunked.com/marbleous-machines)
- [Visnos: interactive Galton board with route counts](https://www.visnos.com/demos/galton-board)

## Design observations to preserve for Swap (no new feature commitment)

1. **Physical identity:** an object stays the same object as the hand moves it.
   Object identity, current slot, dimension, color, and motion history should
   be representable independently.
2. **Invariant under changing the unit:** if rods of lengths `a`, `b`,
   and `c` satisfy `a+b=c`, scaling all lengths by `s > 0` preserves
   the relation. Do not bind the idea to a particular number of centimeters.
3. **Movement is informative:** passing one piece past another can create
   over/under information, unlike just exchanging two slots.
4. **One variable at a time:** traditional Montessori sets often isolate
   length, height, diameter, or outline. Keep this as an interaction/design
   observation, not a mandate for production visuals.
5. **Different axes are different ideas:** graded lengths, countable units,
   geometric area, permutation, and random branch paths are related but
   should not be fused into a single mechanism prematurely.
6. **The exact original material is unresolved.** The user's remembered
   count of twelve bars is not a canonical specification of Montessori Red
   Rods, Number Rods, or Cuisenaire rods: the references above show ten
   graduated lengths. Keep twelve as a user-recalled detail and a possible
   extension, not a verified historical fact.
7. **No image license implied.** Pictures above are remotely linked
   third-party reference photographs, credited to their source pages, not
   checked-in/redistributable image assets for an APK.

The purpose of this note is shared visual reference for later discussions;
the current three-strand braid and flipping triangle remain the first
implementation slice.
